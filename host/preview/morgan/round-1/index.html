Who you are

You are the Blackwoods' lawyer: you draft the wills, bury the scandals, and know where every body in this family is buried, figuratively. Arthur trusts you with everything. Tonight that is a problem.

Your costume

Sharp and expensive: a tailored black suit or severe column dress, pearls, a good watch, and a leather document folio you never put down. Look like you bill for breathing.

Your story

Arthur met you at nineteen, a mill-town girl waiting tables at one of his dinners, and paid for law school "because you looked at the silver like you were pricing it." Now you have a townhouse, a horse named Precedent, and a wardrobe, none of it quite paid for.

Fourteen months ago a property gamble collapsed under you. You "borrowed" $2.4 million from Blackwood Holdings in six transfers, authorized through your own firm and parked in a shell company you named Corvid Holdings because you thought it was funny. You always meant to pay it back.

All week Arthur asked you helpful questions about "odd transfers." You were very helpful.

The Midnight Supper

You have been invited to the Midnight Supper at Blackwood Manor. This page is yours alone. Don't show it to anyone, don't forward the link, and don't let anyone read over your shoulder. Your secrets are worth real (fake) money tonight.

More of this page unlocks as the night goes on. When Reggie breaks the seal on a new round, refresh and your next envelope will be waiting.

The house

Blackwood Manor was built in 1891 by Silas Blackwood out of black stone, coal money and money nobody asks about. Thirty-one rooms in use, a dozen that aren't. The portraits in the gallery are said to watch you. The east wing has been shut for decades. The lights do strange things in a storm.

Rooms you'll hear about tonight: the Drawing Room (the party), the Front Hall, the Portrait Gallery, the Billiard Room, the Conservatory, the Dining Room (set for the Supper), the Terrace, the Library, and at the end of the East Corridor, Arthur's study.

The legend

On Arthur's desk sits the Blackwood Raven, eight pounds of bronze that Silas had cast, people say, from the melted bell of a chapel he tore down. "The raven keeps the Blackwood secrets," the family says.

There's a less polite saying too: every Blackwood who has tried to give the fortune away has died before the papers were signed. Edmund in 1923, down the cellar stairs. Cordelia in 1979, of a fever, an hour before midnight.

And last month the anonymous society column The Keyhole printed a blind item: "Which crumbling pile has a tenant nobody invited? Staff report footsteps inside the walls and a larder that empties itself."

Rules of the Supper

  1. No guest leaves Blackwood Manor before midnight.
  2. One chair is left empty for the last Blackwood to die.
  3. The host's midnight announcement is final. What is said at midnight cannot be unsaid.
  4. Every guest arrives with a secret.
  5. The east wing stays dark after eleven.
  6. Never say the bird's name at the table. Call it the bird.
  7. The last drink before midnight is poured by the house.

Blackmail Cash

Everyone starts with $3,000 in Blackwood Blackmail Notes (three $1,000 bills).

  • Offer another guest $1,000 for one question about their character, whereabouts, secret or knowledge.
  • If they accept the money, they must answer that question truthfully. They don't have to volunteer anything beyond the question.
  • They may refuse, or demand more ($2,000, $3,000…). Refusing costs nothing except everyone now really wants to know why.
  • Reggie pays $1,000 for excellent accusations, found evidence, completed secret missions and committed performances.
  • $3,000 buys a private hint card from Reggie. Hints nudge; they never solve.

How to play

  • Stay in character, flirt, scheme, deflect, accuse. Be dramatic.
  • Don't invent evidence unless your envelope says you may.
  • If your envelope says something must come out, make sure it's public before the round ends.
  • Search only the designated game areas. Nothing is hidden in personal belongings, bedrooms, drawers or cabinets.
  • Never open a future envelope early. (Kushington.)
