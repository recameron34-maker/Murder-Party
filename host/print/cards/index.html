Blackwood Manor · CharacterFor Annie only

Antoinette "Annie"

House manager of Blackwood Manor: You've poured every drink in this house for eleven years. You know who needs one.

You run Blackwood Manor: the cellar keys, the guest lists, the seating plan, the lies. Every Midnight Supper for eleven years you have carried Arthur the Reckoning, his 9:30 Sazerac. Tonight you carried it for the last time.

Costume

Impeccable and slightly old-fashioned: a black dress or waistcoat with a crisp white collar, a ring of antique keys at your hip, and a silver tray you are never seen without. Staff, but the kind who outranks most guests.

Your story

You arrived at nineteen to polish silver and never left. You know which guest takes sugar, which takes a lover, and which takes the spoons home. The family treats you like furniture, which is how you hear everything.

Your sideline is that for three years you have quietly sold Arthur's lesser vintages to a London restaurateur and refilled the bottles with supermarket claret. Arthur never noticed, which tells you everything you need to know about rich men's palates. You also slip a little gossip to Albie, the medium, before her "readings," for a cut.

And you owe Morgan. When your marriage went bad, she handled your divorce for nothing and got you the flat. You don't forget kindness. It's rare enough in this house.

Connections

  • Arthur Blackwood: Your employer for eleven years. Gruff, generous, impossible. You'll miss bringing him that drink more than you'll say.
  • Morgan Leibold: She did your divorce for free. You'd walk into fire for her, or at least lie to Reggie.
  • Aalvia "Albie": You feed her gossip before her "readings." The spirits split the fee with you, fifty-fifty.
  • Shea: The nurse. You two run this house between you. You've noticed she's been taking extra food upstairs at night.
  • Kevin Blackwood: You watched him grab Arthur's coat off the hall hook at 9:22 and storm outside. Typical.

Your evening

  • 8:00 PM–9:29 PM Champagne, canapés, seating-plan diplomacy. Clara and Arthur disappeared into the study at 8:30; you noted the time and her lipstick.
  • 9:22 PM Kevin storms past you, takes the nearest coat off the hook (Arthur's, as it happens) and goes out onto the terrace.
  • 9:30 PM You mix the Reckoning at the bar, Arthur's Sazerac, as the Supper rules require.
  • 9:31 PM You carry it in. Arthur is alive and alone at his desk, writing, a red leather folder open in front of him. He says, "Last one, Annie." You thought he meant the drink.
  • 9:32 PM Walking back down the East Corridor you pass Morgan heading toward the study. She smiles. You smile. She has a meeting; it's none of your business.
  • 9:33 PM–9:47 PM Back to champagne duty. The lights go out at 9:45 with a crash from somewhere downstairs. You're holding a full tray in the dark.

What you know

  • You were the last person to see Arthur alive, at 9:31. He was alone.
  • You passed Morgan in the East Corridor at 9:32, walking toward the study.
  • Kevin left for the terrace at 9:22 wearing Arthur's coat.
  • Someone has been raiding the larder at night. The staff say it's a ghost. You say it's Shea.

Your secret

You saw Morgan heading to the study at 9:32 and you haven't told anyone, out of loyalty. Separately, you've been selling Arthur's wine and refilling the bottles, and you feed Albie gossip for her "visions."

If someone pays you

  • You saw Morgan in the East Corridor at 9:32.
  • You've been selling Arthur's wine and refilling the bottles.
  • Albie's "spirit messages" come from you.

Private instructions

  • Until Round Three, refuse any Blackmail question about who you saw in the East Corridor, whatever the price.
  • Reveal the 9:31 drink early and confidently. It's the most important time of the night.
  • You may pour a drink for anyone who asks you a question. Watch who drinks it.
  • Keep a straight face when people praise the wine.

Dr. Noor Beaumont

Arthur's private physician: You've seen how every body in this house ends. Tonight you were right.

You are Arthur's doctor, discreet and brilliant, and the only person in the house besides his nurse who knows how sick he was. You're married to the Professor, Joji Beaumont, who loves this house more than is healthy, and you're expecting your first child.

Costume

Elegant and clinical, and comfortable: a dark flowing dress or soft tailoring, hair pinned back, reading glasses on a chain, and a vintage doctor's bag that you insist is "purely decorative." Low heels. You're expecting; nobody gets to make you stand all night.

Your story

For six years you've kept Arthur Blackwood alive with excellent medicine and total discretion. He had heart failure: months, not years. He made you promise to tell no one.

The Beaumonts have Blackwood money in their bones. Arthur funds Joji's research on the house, and Joji's research has quietly paid your mortgage. When you told Arthur about the baby he insisted on being godfather and had Annie invent a non-alcoholic Reckoning just for you. "The Reckoning without the reckoning," he called it. You're going to miss him.

You also have a slightly mortifying secret. Last spring Arthur asked you, with enormous dignity, for "something to put the spring back in the step." You prescribed it. You have since become aware that the spring was for Clara. You'd rather die than discuss it, which in this house is a real risk.

Connections

  • Professor Joji Beaumont: Your husband, the Professor. Brilliant, distracted, in love with a building, and about to be a father. He has measured the nursery four times.
  • Arthur Blackwood: Your patient of six years, your patron and a terrible patient. He never once took his pills on time.
  • Shea: Arthur's nurse. You two are the only ones who knew the truth about his heart.
  • Clara: You know far more about her and Arthur's "meetings" than you'd like to, medically speaking.

Your evening

  • 8:00 PM–9:15 PM Trapped by Kushington explaining forensic accounting. You diagnosed him with "enthusiasm" and escaped.
  • 9:15 PM–9:45 PM By the fire with Joji and Hannah, the whole time. Joji was describing load-bearing walls. You were counting the minutes until supper.
  • 9:47 PM When the lights come back and Reggie finds Arthur, you go in with him. One blow to the head. The body is already cooling. That bothers you a great deal.

What you know

  • Arthur had severe heart failure and only months to live. Only you and Shea knew.
  • His illness did not kill him. One blow to the head with something heavy did.
  • From the body (temperature, the drink half-finished, the blood) he died between 9:35 and 9:40 PM, not during the blackout.
  • You prescribed Arthur a little "vitality" medication for reasons involving Clara.

Your secret

You've known since 9:47 that Arthur died before the lights went out, between 9:35 and 9:40. You've kept it to yourself because you wanted to be certain, and because a doctor saying "murder" in this house is how doctors get uninvited. You also prescribed Arthur's "vitality" pills.

If someone pays you

  • Arthur died between 9:35 and 9:40, before the blackout.

  • Be clinical and precise. You are the one person here whose facts nobody should doubt.
  • You may examine any physical evidence Reggie produces and give a medical opinion. You never have to crawl about searching for it; send someone else.
  • Never mention the "vitality" pills unless paid.
Blackwood Manor · CharacterFor Joji only

Professor Joji Beaumont

Architectural historian: Every house is a confession, if you know how to read the walls.

You've spent eight months studying Blackwood Manor for a book, The Walls of Blackwood. You know this house better than the family does, and frankly you like it better than the family too. You're married to Dr. Noor Beaumont, who tolerates this.

Costume

Tweedy academic gone gothic: a velvet jacket or waistcoat, a scarf, ink on your fingers, a rolled-up blueprint or a notebook stuffed with loose sketches. Optional: a measuring tape you produce at inappropriate moments.

Your story

Arthur funds your research. In return you're writing the definitive history of the manor and preparing its application for landmark status. The paperwork went off in August with your full survey, including copies of Silas Blackwood's original 1891 plans. The originals came back to you with somebody's office stamp on them. You were annoyed about the ink.

Those plans show what the family has forgotten. Silas hated seeing his servants, so he built passages inside the walls so they could move unseen. Most were bricked up in the 1950s. You are not certain all of them were.

Your shameful secret: you have pocketed a small brass servants' bell from the east wing, labeled Study. It's on your desk at home. You meant to give it back. You won't.

Connections

  • Dr. Noor Beaumont: Your wife, the doctor, who is expecting your first child. She's the only person who can make you stop talking about cornices. You have already designed the baby a library.
  • Arthur Blackwood: Your patron. He loved the house's stories almost as much as you do, and he guarded some of them.
  • Timothy "Tim": The photographer. He keeps asking good questions about the library. You like him.
  • Louis: He has been pestering you for weeks about the "Raven Room" and wants to see your plans. You have refused, on principle and because he smells faintly of dust.
  • Alma: The archivist. She leaves notes in library books for you. You suspect she has a crush. You are flattered and terrified.

Your evening

  • 8:00 PM–8:40 PM Giving an unrequested tour of the portraits. Louis hung on every word about hidden rooms.
  • 8:40 PM–9:15 PM Holding court on the manor's "architectural secrets." People pretended to listen.
  • 9:15 PM–9:45 PM By the fire with Noor and Hannah the whole time, explaining load-bearing walls with hand gestures.

What you know

  • Silas Blackwood built servants' passages inside the walls in 1891. Most were sealed in the 1950s.
  • The 1891 plans show a faint line between the study and the library that you've never been able to explain.
  • The family legend of a hidden "Raven Room" that appears on no plan.
  • Copies of your survey and the plans went out with the landmark paperwork in August and came back stamped.

Your secret

You suspect, though you've never proven it, that a passage connects Arthur's study to the library. Arthur forbade you to investigate the study wall. Also, you stole a servants' bell.

If someone pays you
